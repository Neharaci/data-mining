INTERPRETATION
--------------
The skew is visible both in the notice population and in candidate
generation. A relatively small group of portals contributes a large
fraction of retrieval work.

The mechanical reason is that each notice participates in LSH
candidate generation. Portals with disproportionately many notices
therefore create more candidate lookups and pair comparisons.
Similar or repetitive portal content can also increase bucket
collisions and candidate generation.

20-MINUTE REQUIREMENT
---------------------
The measured full-corpus LSH candidate-generation stage at the
0.55 operating point took 18.885 seconds on the test machine.
This is substantially below the 20-minute nightly requirement.

The measurement covers candidate generation and not an end-to-end
production ingestion/rebuild pipeline, so the remaining pipeline
stages must also be budgeted before claiming a complete nightly
20-minute guarantee.

MITIGATION
----------
Use the LSH structure for sublinear candidate retrieval and retain
the exact normalized-word Jaccard >= 0.65 decision as the final
merge rule.

For skewed portals, use portal-aware batching/sharding of retrieval
work so that the highest-volume portals do not create a single
serial bottleneck. Process portal partitions independently and
combine their candidate results.

The mitigation preserves the same retrieval threshold and exact
merge threshold, so retrieval quality is not intentionally traded
away merely to handle portal skew.

RETRIEVAL-QUALITY PRICE
-----------------------
At LSH threshold 0.55:

True labelled same-pair survival:
221 / 279 = 79.21%

Labelled different pairs surviving:
147 / 621

Candidate reduction:
85.497%

Thus the skew mitigation is applied to execution organization rather
than by raising the LSH threshold, because raising the threshold
would reduce candidate workload but would also lose more true
duplicate pairs.
